There are times in everyone's life when they run into minor problems or inconveniences for which no solution exists, or if one does exist, it is far more than they would be willing to pay. Learning even the basics of CAD and having access to a 3D printer (owning one or using a service) can be incredibly useful in solving niche issues. This article showcases how you can go from a problem to a solution following a creative design process.

### Step 1: Finding a problem
Step one is often the easiest step in the process, as you usually already know what you want a solution to. Throughout this article, I will follow the process of creating a simple clip-on dirt cover for my car's front seat to replace the one that broke a few years back. 

Although this part is available directly from the manufacturer, it would have to be purchased in pairs with other parts, driving up the price for something that could be designed and manufactured in only a few hours in the comfort of my own home. 

[![20241105-101717.jpg](https://i.postimg.cc/xdt1F6Fc/20241105-101717.jpg)](https://postimg.cc/v1xy4tys)

Above is a picture of the area the cover is intended for. This area is prone to capturing dirt that is not easy to remove using a standard vacuum or shop vacuum, and thus, a cover used to exist there to protect dirt from getting in. This project aims to create a simple cover that clips in the same way the existing part did and prevents additional dirt from getting into this area. 

### Step 2: Getting your measurements
Now that you know what you want to accomplish, it is time to measure as much as possible. Your measurements do not have to be done a certain way, but it is wise to make measurements that will aid you in the design step, such as measuring the distance of midpoints from one side or measuring depth.
For some applications, a ruler may be sufficient for good measurements, but it is often recommended that a precision measuring tool such as calipers be used to ensure accurate results. 

[![Screenshot-20241105-095254-Samsung-Notes.png](https://i.postimg.cc/k5gPpqSd/Screenshot-20241105-095254-Samsung-Notes.png)](https://postimg.cc/qNSFtfQm)

Once you have your measurements, write them down as a rough drawing, such as the one above. Your drawing does not have to be at scale or even completely legible; as long as you understand what is shown, you are good enough to move on to the next step. 

### Step 3: Designing your part

[![Screenshot-2024-09-28-125649.png](https://i.postimg.cc/x83PhkBf/Screenshot-2024-09-28-125649.png)](https://postimg.cc/5QjvFtxT)

During this step, you will finally turn your drawing into a reality. Boot up your favorite CAD software (in this case, I am using Solidworks), and create your model.
If you need to learn how to use CAD, I have attached a detailed document on using a free web-based software called [OnShape](https://www.onshape.com/en/). Although this was initially made to aid in teaching CAD for robotics, the concepts carry over. You can also find a large variety of resources on your chosen CAD software's website or YouTube. 

During this step, you may find it challenging to complete your model fully or need more clarification on a dimension. If you find yourself in this situation, the best thing you can do is make the part wrong a few times so you can see what needs to be changed rather than overthink things in CAD. Although you may waste more material doing this, you will often reach a better result with less time in the design process.

### Step 4: Manufacturing

[![20240928-143252.jpg](https://i.postimg.cc/Pq6cP7qb/20240928-143252.jpg)](https://postimg.cc/HJy3NBXj)

Once your design is done, it is time to transfer it from the computer to the real world. There are several ways to do this, including 3D printing, carpentry, CNC, and many more. The process you decide to use depends entirely on your aim.
For this project, I chose to 3D print my part, which enabled me to create a near-perfect copy of what I created in CAD. When creating 3D-printed parts, it is essential to consider the material you are printing in, as it will determine the properties of your finished product. 
Some examples of 3D printer filament materials:
- **PLA** - The most common printer filament, PLA is easy to print and strong enough for most applications. However, it can be brittle and break easily under stress. 
- **PETG**—Similar to PLA, this filament is easy to print. However, it is better in many ways, such as temperature resistance and being slightly flexible, making it less susceptible to breakage. 
- **ASA/ABS**—ASA and ABS are both materials that can be harder to print and are prone to warping. However, these materials are excellent for higher-stress scenarios where temperature resistance is needed. 
- **Polycarbonate filaments**—This family of filaments is generally quite challenging to print and is best suited for applications requiring strong materials.
For this project, I chose to 3D print my part using PLA because it is low-cost and reliable. 

### Step 5: Testing and redesigning
As any sound engineer would tell you, no design is ever truly complete. As you continue to use your new solution, you may run into problems you would like to address; the good thing about this is that once you have a functional prototype, making modifications is easy. 

[![20240928-143634.jpg](https://i.postimg.cc/02Y9b81p/20240928-143634.jpg)](https://postimg.cc/bsw75fJJ)

For this example, one issue I ran into was that the clips holding the part in place broke when I was installing it. There were several ways I could have gone about fixing this—changing filaments, changing print orientation so the part would not break at the layer lines, and much more. However, I opted for making the clips thinner and increasing the wall thickness in the print settings, making the part able to withstand more flex before breaking.
